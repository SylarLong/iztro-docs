# Zi Wei Dou Shu: Palace system（紫微斗數宮位系統）   {#紫微斗数宫位系统}

## Introduction   {#前言}

A palace, also called an `enclosure`（垣）, is one of the twelve squares arranged around a Zi Wei Dou Shu chart according to the Earthly Branches. A `palace` and a `position` are different concepts: a `palace` has a fixed name, whereas a `position` is defined relative to a reference palace. Consider your `dad` and `grandpa`: these are `absolute relationship names` relative to you. Your father is represented by your `parents`（父母） palace. Your grandfather is represented by your `spirit`（福德） palace; viewed from your `parents`（父母） palace, he occupies its `parents position`（父母位）.

Apart from the soul palace（命宮）, the other 11 palace labels usually omit the word `palace`. The diagram above shows both the `palace name` and the `Earthly Branch`（地支）. Branch positions stay fixed, but the `palace name` assigned to each position varies between people. We therefore use two naming systems. A name formed from `Earthly Branch`（地支） + `palace`, such as `mao palace`（卯宮） or `xu palace`（戌宮）, always refers to the same place in the chart. A `palace name`, by contrast, can occupy different positions in different charts. For example, one person's `wealth`（財帛） palace might be in the `chen palace`（辰宮）, while another person's `wealth`（財帛） palace is in the `shen palace`（申宮）. These two naming systems also give rise to two ways of classifying palaces. Before exploring those classifications, let us first learn about the twelve palaces.

:::tip Tips

The explanations here concern the `natal chart`（本命盤）. It reflects `attitudes` or `outlook`, rather than ideas that are inherently good or bad: we tend to favor views that match our own and dislike those that do not. Such judgments are relative. On that basis, the `natal chart`（本命盤） alone does not show whether a particular time will be fortunate or unfortunate. To examine a palace's condition during a specific period, consult a `horoscope-period chart`（運限盤）. We introduce these in [Horoscope periods](./horoscope.md).

:::

## `soul`（命宮）   {#命宫}

The soul palace（命宮） shows the chart owner's basic character, much like a character class in a game. But personality has many dimensions, so it describes one's `essence` rather than every aspect of behavior. You may have heard that `Character determines destiny`. In the author's view, however, `understanding` matters more: people with similar personalities can see the same situation differently because their understanding differs. We should therefore avoid judging an entire chart as `good` or `poor`, `favorable` or `unfavorable`, using rigid traditional categories. Although the soul palace shows intrinsic traits, the first impression others receive often comes from the [`surface`（遷移）](#迁移宫).

## `siblings`（兄弟）   {#兄弟宫}

The siblings palace（兄弟宮） primarily describes relationships with brothers and sisters. When distinguishing `father` from `mother`, it also helps describe the relationship with one's `mother`. Why the mother? This follows the distinction between `palace` and `position` explained in the [introduction](#前言). The chart owner's `siblings`（兄弟） palace is also, relative to their `parents`（父母） palace, its `spouse position`（夫妻位）. Once familiar with this method, you can derive other relationships. Traditional practitioners also consider the `siblings`（兄弟） palace when examining finances, because relative to the `wealth`（財帛） palace it occupies the `property position`（田宅位）, giving a view of available funds. Avoid repeatedly extending these relationships without a clear purpose: the reasoning can quickly become confusing. First understand the palace's primary role in sibling relationships, then explore further interpretations.

## `spouse`（夫妻）   {#夫妻宫}

The spouse palace（夫妻宮） represents `relationships`, showing attitudes toward relationships and choosing a partner. Although it is outside the soul palace's `trine and opposite palaces`（三方四正）, relationships and marriage can still shape an entire life. This is why balance across all twelve palaces matters more than one outstanding palace. If you are unsure what kind of person attracts you, your chart may offer a reference. `chart comparison for compatibility`（合盤） means comparing both partners' soul and spouse palaces to examine whether their values and relationship attitudes fit. Contrary to a common assumption, the spouse palace is not the palace of `romantic attraction`（桃花）; `romantic attraction`（桃花） is chiefly represented by the `children`（子女） palace.

## `children`（子女）   {#子女宫}

In the spouse-palace discussion, we described the children palace（子女宮） as the palace of `romantic attraction`（桃花）. This association comes from intimacy leading to childbirth: before becoming parents, a couple has no children. Besides `children` and `romantic attraction`（桃花）, this palace can also describe relationships with `business partners`. That is because the `children`（子女） palace, relative to the `career`（官祿） palace, occupies the `friends position`（僕役位）, indicating `work partners`.

## `wealth`（財帛）   {#财帛宫}

Many people assume a strong wealth palace（財帛宮） guarantees good financial fortune, but it primarily shows attitudes toward money, financial management and material desires. It represents the `sustenance` needed for the chart owner's life. A prominent wealth palace suggests greater material needs: more resources may be needed to maintain the same standard of living. This can encourage skill in money management and sensitivity to earning opportunities. The saying `knowing how to spend can motivate you to earn` describes such people. The actual accumulation of wealth is shown by the [`property`（田宅） palace](#田宅宫).

## `health`（疾厄）   {#疾厄宫}

The health palace（疾厄宮） concerns the chart owner's `body` and physical constitution. Many charting applications call it the `health palace`, but that label is incomplete: a healthy person may endure physical strain, while someone who is ill may receive exceptional care. The Chinese name translated here as `health`（疾厄） encompasses both `illness` and `physical hardship`. There is no need to fear the phrase `physical hardship`. Even enjoyable `extreme sports` can be a form of `physical hardship` for the body. Long working days, six or seven days a week with virtually no rest, or work exposed to wind and sun can also be `physical hardship`. This palace also reflects how people treat their bodies, offering clues to why some maintain an exercise routine and manage their physique while others gain weight easily.

:::warning Note

If you feel unwell, seek medical care promptly. Do not neglect or overstrain your body just because your health palace（疾厄宮） appears `strong`.

:::

## `surface`（遷移）   {#迁移宫}

The surface palace（遷移宮） acts as an `entry point` into the chart. People present an outward image to protect themselves, and the `surface`（遷移） shows that `protective outward image` or `self-presentation`. This explains remarks such as 'You were different when we first met': over time, others see through the `surface`（遷移） to the `soul`（命宮） and even the `spirit`（福德）. A strong surface palace suggests skill in presenting oneself and building a public persona, which often brings favorable first impressions. A weaker showing can indicate being `less socially adept`, `casual about appearance`, or having little `approachability`. A casual person who pays less attention to others' opinions may therefore attract more labels or distance from others.

## `friends`（僕役）   {#仆役宫}

Also known as the `friendship palace`（交友宮）, the friends palace（僕役宮） shows your `social circle` and `approach to choosing friends`: what kinds of friends you prefer, how you choose them, and whether your circle contains influential people or mostly companions for eating, drinking and entertainment. These are mainly read from the `friends`（僕役） palace. This palace receives relatively little attention: people ask about work, relationships, money, parents, children and health, but rarely about friends. Friendship involves considerable freedom of choice, and this palace does not identify one particular friend, which helps explain that neglect. Yet the `friends`（僕役） palace also concerns `communication`. We discuss this under [Classification by palace name](#按宫名分类), in the `social line`（兄友線）.

## `career`（官祿）   {#官禄宫}

The career palace（官祿宮）, also called the `career palace`（事業宮）, reflects your `approach to getting things done` and attitude toward work. If you have no clear career plan, it can offer a direction and help you identify the skills needed for the kind of work you hope to do. Does it matter if you have not started working, manage the home full-time, or rely financially on a partner? Yes: it concerns your `approach to handling tasks`, whether or not you have paid employment. For students, `studying` is their daily work; for full-time homemakers, it is `caring for the family`. For someone supported by a partner, maintaining that financial support becomes their daily concern.

## `property`（田宅）   {#田宅宫}

The property palace（田宅宮） concerns `holding and accumulation`. Besides `real estate`, it represents relationships with `family`. Traditional interpretations value `domestic harmony` highly and therefore look to this palace when discussing fortune and misfortune. A harmonious home can support life greatly; if the home situation represented here is particularly difficult, living independently may be worth considering. As explained under the [`wealth`（財帛） palace](#财帛宫), the property palace also shows wealth accumulation because it acts as a `storehouse of wealth`. A strong wealth palace with a disrupted property palace can describe someone who earns a great deal but spends a great deal too. A weaker wealth palace with a strong property palace may instead describe someone who manages income and expenses carefully and enjoys saving. These differences do not make one chart `good` and another `bad`; they reflect different attitudes toward spending and holding resources.

## `spirit`（福德）   {#福德宫}

The surface palace（遷移宮） represents outward presentation, like a character's `cosmetic appearance` in a game. The soul palace（命宮） shows underlying traits, like `initial attribute points`: whether a character is `strength-focused`, `intellect-focused`, or `agility-focused`. The health palace（疾厄宮） concerns bodily strength, comparable to physical resistance. The `spirit`（福德） palace represents `thoughts`. When practitioners advise you to `cultivate inner resilience`, they mean developing inner resilience and emotional stability, rather than letting external circumstances or personal setbacks dictate your reactions. This reduces impulsive or mistaken decisions in response to outside pressure. Other palaces often express `subconscious` responses, while the `spirit`（福德） palace concerns considered responses. Strengthening this inner stability can help you handle difficulties and recover from mistakes.

## `parents`（父母）   {#父母宫}

In this framework, the parents palace（父母宮） is the palace that represents the generation `before` the chart owner, shown by the soul palace（命宮）. It concerns the family into which we are born, a relationship we cannot choose. It describes the parents, most often the `father`. As discussed under the [`siblings`（兄弟） palace](#兄弟宫), the relationship with the `mother` is examined through the `siblings`（兄弟） palace. The parents palace represents `support present from birth`. If the person is a tree, this palace is the soil feeding the seedling: some have rich soil and abundant support, while others grow in poor conditions. Many people would like to be born into privilege and start life with every advantage, but that is not everyone's experience. Zi Wei Dou Shu emphasizes the balance of the whole chart. If the parents palace dominates, other areas are relatively weaker; once parental support is removed, the person may face greater challenges than others.

---

:::tip Tips
A Zi Wei Dou Shu `natal chart`（先天盤） is not inherently good or bad; it describes different attributes. People live together and need cooperation, which requires a variety of skills and personalities. Do not let someone else's subjective judgment of your chart undermine your outlook. Learning the basics yourself can help you understand yourself more fully and objectively.
:::

## Special palaces {#特殊宫位}

Besides the palaces shown in the chart's twelve squares, Zi Wei Dou Shu uses three additional, hidden palaces: the `body`（身宮） palace, the `hidden partner palace`（暗合宮）, and the `origin`（來因） palace.

### `body`（身宮）   {#身宫}

The body palace（身宮） shows the `direction of change` of the chart owner's personality and influences the traits of the `soul`（命宮） palace. The soul palace concerns `innate traits`, whereas the `body`（身宮） palace concerns `acquired traits`. Think of a game character's starting attributes, for example:

| Attribute | Initial value |
| --- | --- |
| Strength | 8 |
| Agility | 4 |
| Intellect | 5 |

The starting attributes are fixed when the character is created. The `body`（身宮） palace is like the choice of where to allocate points gained while leveling up: some prioritize `agility`, others `strength` or `intellect`. Likewise, people whose soul palace contains `emperor`（紫微） may develop differently depending on the major star in the body palace. One may become more domineering, like a tyrant; another more open-minded, like a wise ruler. The `body`（身宮） palace can occupy only these six palaces:

- body palace in the soul palace（身宮在命宮）

    When the body palace（身宮） and soul palace（命宮） share a position, convictions tend to be firmer and personality more stable. In the game analogy, someone starting as a `strength`-based character is likely to keep investing in `strength` as they develop. Choosing a sound direction is therefore especially important: persistence can support steady progress toward success, but a mistaken direction can turn the same persistence into stubborn refusal to change course.

- body palace in the surface palace（身宮在遷移宮）

    People with the body palace（身宮） in the surface palace（遷移宮） care strongly about how others see them and about their `outward presentation`. In the game analogy, they pay more attention to the character's `cosmetic appearance`. They also prefer being out and about. Building an image that earns others' respect can become a lifelong goal, so external recognition matters greatly. If pursued in a sound direction, this may help them `earn recognition`; if pursued through inappropriate methods, it can instead damage their reputation.

- body palace in the spouse palace（身宮在夫妻宮）

    When the body palace（身宮） lies in the spouse palace（夫妻宮）, relationships tend to receive greater emphasis. Building a fulfilling relationship may become a central `life task`. Emotions are among the most complex aspects of life. Strong attachment can make someone `become trapped by relationship worries` and more vulnerable to `emotional manipulation`. Some people therefore withdraw from `love` after being deeply hurt, despite longing for a lasting bond; this is an instinctive form of `self-protection`. When the relationship feels right, however, they may `feel content with love even when money is scarce`, and they may willingly give up other priorities for it.

- body palace in the career palace（身宮在官祿宮）

    With the body palace（身宮） in the career palace（官祿宮）, people pay particular attention to their `behavior` and the `way` in which they complete a task. This develops into a strong focus on `work` or `career`. Because `career` is central to their goals, they are more likely to become a `workaholic` and devote themselves fully to achievement. But if their skills are insufficient or their efforts misdirected, setbacks at work can affect them more deeply than others.

- body palace in the wealth palace（身宮在財帛宮）

    People with the body palace（身宮） in the wealth palace（財帛宮） have stronger material needs and find security in wealth. They are therefore more prone to `preoccupation with money`. Most people value money, but this placement puts particular emphasis on it. The traditional ideal is to earn it through proper means. Excessive attachment to relationships, work or wealth can be unhelpful; here it is especially tempting because money and material resources are tangible, encouraging the urge to control, possess and compete for them.

- body palace in the spirit palace（身宮在福德宮）

    With the body palace（身宮） in the spirit palace（福德宮）, people place greater value on `inner` fulfillment. As explained under the [`spirit`（福德） palace](#福德宫), this concerns inner experience and thought. The placement strengthens that inward focus and desire for enjoyment. It can also increase `susceptibility to dependence`, making someone more dependent on the people, activities or objects they like. As with the question of `direction of pursuit` in other body-palace placements, pursuing an unhelpful path here can lead to particularly difficult problems. These people therefore need to pay greater attention to their psychological state.

`hidden pairing`（暗合） describes a connection working `behind the scenes`. In the `visible recognition and hidden prosperity`（明科暗祿） pattern, the `soul`（命宮） palace contains transformation `C`（化科）, while its `hidden partner palace`（暗合宮） contains transformation `A`（化祿） or `money`（祿存）. This combines visible `fame` with less visible `wealth`. The author links its favorable reputation to a traditional Chinese preference for public recognition accompanied by quiet prosperity. This is why `visible recognition and hidden prosperity`（明科暗祿） is a well-regarded pattern. We discuss patterns in [Chart patterns](./pattern.md#紫微斗数格局).

### `origin`（來因）   {#来因宫}

The origin palace（來因宮） considers the question of `why one has come into this life`. Treat it as a reference: it is used relatively little in practice, and it is enough here simply to know the concept exists.

- What is the origin palace（來因宮） used for?

    The origin palace（來因宮） can offer a reference when choosing a field of work. In the `children`（子女） palace, it may suggest work involving `parents and children`, `social interaction`, or `mergers and acquisitions`. In the `wealth`（財帛） palace, examples include `finance`, `securities`, and `investment`. These are possibilities to consider, not a rule for deciding your career.

- How do you find the origin palace（來因宮）?

    Find the palace whose stem matches the birth year's `Heavenly Stem`（天干）. For someone born in a `gui-mao`（癸卯） year, the palace with stem `gui`（癸） is the origin palace（來因宮）. The `zi`（子） and `chou`（丑） positions are excluded; this rule is called `heaven and earth are excluded as origins`（天地不做來因）. As explained in [Terminology](./basis.md#术语解释), the `zi palace`（子宮） represents heaven and the `chou palace`（丑宮） represents earth. If the matching stem falls at either of these positions, use the `yin palace`（寅宮） or `mao palace`（卯宮） instead, respectively. These share their stems with zi and chou.

## Classification of palaces（宮位）   {#宫位的分类}

### Classification by Earthly Branch {#按地支分类}

Each palace position has distinct properties that add tendencies to the chart owner's character. This classification comes from the five elements（五行）. In this model, `⛰️Earth` is the `final resting place` of all material things: `🪙Metal`, `🪵Wood`, `🌊Water`, and `🔥Fire` ultimately return to `⛰️Earth`. On that basis, the positions fall into three groups:

#### four growth positions   {#四生地}

These are also called the `four horse positions`（四馬地） because `horse`（天馬） appears only in these four positions. Excluding `⛰️Earth`, they mark the `birth` of the other four elements. Like energetic, curious children who want to explore, they add `ability to take action` and a `desire to explore`, but their limited stability can also make them `changeable`. The positions are:

- yin（寅）

    `🪵Wood`: growth position

- shen（申）

    `🪙Metal`: growth position

- si（巳）

    `🔥Fire`: growth position

- hai（亥）

    `🌊Water`: growth position

#### four storage positions   {#四墓库}

After childhood `curiosity` and the `confidence` of one's prime comes the `composure` of middle age. These four positions add `steadiness` and `concentration`, but reduce some `vitality` and `drive`. The `money`（祿存） star does not occupy them, hence the traditional rule `祿存遇土不進`. We introduce it under [The money star（祿存）](./minor-star.md#禄存). It symbolizes `wealth`; the author takes this as a reminder that resources need `flow`, a connection he admires as an example of the ancients’ insight. The four storage positions are:

- chen（辰）

- xu（戌）

    `🔥Fire`: storage position

- chou（丑）

    `🪙Metal`: storage position

- wei（未）

### Classification by palace name   {#按宫名分类}
Classifying palaces by Earthly Branch describes tendencies added to the chart owner's character. Classifying them by palace name concerns `life matters` instead. The twelve palaces form six paired lines based on the [`six oppositions of the Earthly Branches`（地支六衝）](./basis.md#地支六冲). Each pair is an `opposing pair`: its two palaces are closely connected, supporting one another while also creating tensions. The six lines are:

- fate line（命遷線）

    This line connects the `soul`（命宮） and `surface`（遷移） palaces, representing the person's `inner nature and outward presentation`. Palaces interacting with this line therefore directly affect the chart owner.

- social line（兄友線）

    This line connects the `siblings`（兄弟） and `friends`（僕役） palaces and represents `social interaction`. Because social interaction depends on communication, it is also called the `communication line`（溝通線）. A person's social network reflects the circles in which they can act and develop, which is why another name is the `achievement line`（成就線）.

- career line（官夫線）

    This line connects the `career`（官祿） and `spouse`（夫妻） palaces and concerns `career`. A supportive partner can help your working life; a partner who is dissatisfied with or opposed to your work can make it harder. Your career situation also affects your standing within the relationship. Balancing the two sides is therefore difficult: too much focus on work can neglect the relationship, while too much focus on the relationship can slow professional progress and give an impression of limited ambition. Handling this balance well can make a substantial difference to your life.

- children and property line（子田線）

    This line connects the `children`（子女） and `property`（田宅） palaces. It is also called the `romance line`（桃花線）; see the [`children`（子女） palace](#子女宫) for the explanation. Its scope includes parent–child relationships, family relationships, partnerships and relationships with the opposite sex.

- financial line（財福線）

    This line connects the `wealth`（財帛） and `spirit`（福德） palaces, chiefly reflecting attitudes toward spending and financial management.

- parents and health line（父疾線）

    This line connects the `parents`（父母） and `health`（疾厄） palaces. It is also called the `documents line`（文書線）, because it represents the relationship between the chart owner and their parents in terms of `a contractual bond`.

:::tip Tips

Remembering these `alternative names` helps with chart interpretation. For example, if a transformation D（化忌） from the `spouse`（夫妻） palace opposes the `social line`（兄友線）, its alternative name—the `communication line`（溝通線）—suggests that `communication problems` may be present in the person's relationship.

:::

## trine and opposite palaces（三方四正）   {#三方四正}

This term refers to four palaces together, not one palace: the `target palace`（本宮）, the `opposite palace`（對宮） (also called the `opposite position`（四正位）), and the two `trine palaces`（三合宮） (also called `trine positions`（三方位）). Because the `trine positions`（三方位） and `opposite position`（四正位） strongly affect the target palace, they are analyzed together.

- target palace（本宮）

    The `target palace` is whichever palace you are currently examining.

- opposite palace（對宮）

    The branch of the `target palace`（本宮） forms one of the [`six oppositions`（六衝）](./basis.md#地支六冲) with another branch. The palace at that other branch lies, relative to the `target palace`, directly `opposite`, hence its name, `opposite palace`（對宮）. For example, the palace opposite the `yin palace`（寅宮） is the `shen palace`（申宮）.

- trine palaces（三合宮）

    These are the two other palaces whose branches join the `target palace` in one of the [`Earthly Branch trine groups`（地支三合局）](./basis.md#地支三合局). For example, the trine palaces of the `shen palace`（申宮） are the `zi palace`（子宮） and the `chen palace`（辰宮）.
